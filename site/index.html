The story

09:14

Northgate Bank runs AI agents over commercial loan files. At seq 217 (2026-11-03 09:14), commercial-loan-agent approves a £2,000,000 facility for Meridian Holdings. Later, an engineer with database access opens the table and flips decision to declined.

09:16

Nothing looks wrong. No alert fires. The dashboard is green, because the dashboard is reading the same table that was just edited.

09:31

Earlier, the audit store went down. A contractor asked for administrator rights (seq 212): the platform refused — no record, no grant. A departing employee's role was revoked in the same window (seq 214): that completed and waited in a durable queue, because blocking a removal would be worse than the outage.

D+1

The auditor runs one command. It prints divergence at 217. She does not ask the bank for anything, and she does not need their cooperation to check the rest.

The engineer could recompute every hash from 217 to 500 afterwards. It still would not help: a signed Ed25519 seal and create-only anchor were already written somewhere the bank's administrators cannot delete.

Two questions, two apps

Neither question is enough on its own. A record that is provably unchanged is still useless if the action never reached it; a complete record is worthless if anyone with a database login can rewrite it.
